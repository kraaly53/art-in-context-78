## Dates and attribution

Banksy’s birthdate and identity are unconfirmed. Heather Galler’s birthdate was not established from reliable sources. Morrisseau’s birthyear varies between sources; the display acknowledges that uncertainty. Kamal-ol-Molk’s birthyear is approximate. I.Rice Pereira uses 1902, correcting dates that circulated in older material. Primary sources confirm that Hockney and Kusama died in 2026; the biographies use past tense.

The review corrected Thiebaud’s Cakes to 1963, identified Love is in the Bin as a partly shredded canvas painting rather than a print, and distinguished Harrison’s 1967 arrival in Canada from his 1968 settlement in Carcross. Vast Yukon’s 2009 donation is not presented as a creation date. For Britto, sources differ on the year of an Absolut campaign; the timeline uses late 1980s and gives representative collection titles without invented dates. Heather Galler’s entries are representative bodies of work rather than invented famous single objects.

## Classroom framing

For Grade 7–8, students are asked to distinguish observation, sourced fact, and interpretation; identify a claim and supporting evidence; compare artists across media and historical settings; and make their own response with a clear intention and audience.

Indigenous cultures are living and distinct. Morrisseau is identified as Anishinaabe. Carr’s inspiration is contextualised without suggesting she was Indigenous. Activities ask for students’ own symbols, and warn against treating culturally specific sacred imagery as decoration. Remington is one selective view of the American West, not a neutral account of all its people. Appropriation, patronage, social exclusion, and consumer culture are introduced as questions for critical discussion.

No graphic biographical tragedy or sexual biography is included. Some art discusses war, inequality, disability, race, anxiety, or protest in age-appropriate terms. The Dalí sculpture photograph includes nonsexual figurative anatomy; a teacher should preview it along with the rest of the collection. A work may be described without showing an unavailable or unsuitable reproduction.

Suggested lesson:5 minutes introduce a question;10 minutes investigate one profile and its work studies;10 minutes compare with another artist;10 minutes respond to a creative brief;5 minutes write a claim supported by visual evidence and context. Assessment can focus on reasoning and thoughtful choices rather than trivia recall or drawing realism.
