# Canadian classroom image use

## Purpose and scope

The owner confirmed that this resource is for a Canadian classroom. The teacher wants students to study a curated set of approved material instead of searching the open web. The Grade 7–8 activities ask students to analyse artworks, compare choices and consider social and historical context. There are no advertisements, accounts, external student links or image-export tools.

The earlier open-licence-only approach was too restrictive. An absent open licence is not, by itself, a finding that an educational reproduction is unlawful. This edition also includes copyrighted illustrations assessed for Canadian fair dealing. It does not label those images public domain or claim the artist gave permission.

## Applicable provisions

[Copyright Act s.29](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-42/section-29.html) includes education among fair-dealing purposes. [Section 29.1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-42/section-29.1.html) addresses criticism/review and requires source and, when supplied, author attribution. Fairness must still be assessed; “educational” is not an automatic exemption for every act of copying.

[Section 30.04](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-42/section-30.04.html) is a separate exception for online material used by an educational institution or someone acting under its authority. Its conditions include attribution, an audience primarily consisting of students or people acting under the institution’s authority, no relevant access/copy restrictions, no explicit prohibitory notice beyond a copyright symbol alone, and no knowledge or reason to know that the source posting was unauthorized. We do **not** assume those conditions merely because a picture is online. This project’s fair-dealing assessments do not depend on an unverified claim that all s.30.04 conditions are met.

## How the assessed images are used

Each added copyrighted illustration has its own title, date where verified, creator/source attribution, descriptive alt text, and original work-specific visual commentary or a question. The same commentary accompanies the enlarged viewer. The image is part of the lesson rather than a decorative replacement for the original.

New fair-dealing images are limited to a maximum of 640 pixels on the long edge. The site does not provide the original-resolution source files, a bulk image downloader or image-only export. Whole compositions may be necessary to analyse relationships between forms; cropping can remove the very evidence students are asked to discuss. Ten works per artist is a learning target, **not** a statutory copying allowance.

The image records consider purpose, character/distribution, amount, alternatives, nature and potential market effect. See the [Canadian university explanation of this fairness framework](https://copyright.uwinnipeg.ca/fair-dealing/two-tests-for-determining-fair-dealing-.html). Full image records and individual assessments are preserved in IMAGE-CREDITS.json. A reduced-size image is not automatically fair, and attribution alone does not establish fairness.

## Public hosting and limits

GitHub Pages **and the repository are public**. The classroom purpose does not turn them into a password-protected learning platform. Public distribution weighs against fairness and is included in the assessment; reduced image sizes, original commentary and absence of commercial use weigh in the other direction. Search-engine exclusion requests reduce discoverability but do not restrict access or stop copying. Browser users can still save images.

No paywall or access control is bypassed, and no source’s restricted-use contract is accepted on the owner’s behalf. A source copyright notice is preserved where available. Source illustrations are not represented as openly licensed simply because they were displayed on another site. Any direct permission, institution-held licence or applicable public-domain basis remains preferable when available.

This is the project’s documented working assessment, not a court ruling or a school-board clearance. The school’s own policy and any applicable agreements still matter. Rehosting for another audience, commercial reuse, larger images or redistribution as an image library requires a fresh assessment. The MIT/CC0 terms for original code/text do not grant rights to third-party images.
