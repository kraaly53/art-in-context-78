School suitability takes priority over fame: a work containing nudity, violence or other excluded content is replaced with a suitable well-known or representative work. ## Jonathan Green: Zebra Farm Bus
A museum-collected, repeatedly reproduced Gullah-community painting selected for the Fralin’s Writer’s Eye teaching programme. No reliable source establishes one objectively most-famous Green painting; this is an explicitly representative, museum-documented choice rather than a recent portfolio upload.
Sources: https://uvafralinartmuseum.virginia.edu/system/files/WE24%20Self-Guide.pdf · https://www.jonathangreenstudios.com/about

## William H. Johnson: Jitterbugs (III)
The Jitterbugs dancers belong to Johnson’s widely recognized signature series discussed by MoMA, the Met and Smithsonian. This existing III screenprint represents that major series; the sources do not establish one uniquely most-famous individual Johnson work.
Sources: https://www.moma.org/artists/22989-william-h-johnson · https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/491485 · https://americanart.si.edu/exhibitions/johnson

## Leonardo da Vinci: Mona Lisa
The Louvre identifies this as the world’s most famous painting; a strong, unambiguous lead image for Leonardo.
Sources: https://www.louvre.fr/en/explore/the-palace/from-the-mona-lisa-to-the-wedding-feast-at-cana

## Raphael: Portrait of Baldassare Castiglione
A widely recognised Raphael portrait in the Louvre, selected in place of The School of Athens because the full fresco includes a nude statue. Classroom suitability takes priority over an absolute fame ranking.
Sources: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Baldassare_Castiglione,_by_Raffaello_Sanzio,_from_C2RMF_retouched.jpg

## Banksy: Girl with Balloon
Moco Museum names Girl with Balloon among Banksy’s most recognisable works. Use the canonical heart-balloon composition already in the gallery, not a different mural or the later partially shredded auction object.
Sources: https://www.mocomuseum.com/news/amsterdam/the-most-famous-banksy-artworks-explained/

## Paul Cézanne: The Basket of Apples
A major Cézanne still life held by the Art Institute of Chicago, selected in place of The Card Players because that image contains a smoking pipe. This school-appropriate feature represents his influential still-life practice.
Sources: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Paul_C%C3%A9zanne_-_The_Basket_of_Apples_-_1926.252_-_Art_Institute_of_Chicago.jpg

## Frida Kahlo: The Frame (painting)
The Centre Pompidou’s distinctive framed self-portrait is a well-known, fully clothed Kahlo work. It replaces The Two Fridas because that painting shows exposed hearts and blood. Classroom suitability takes priority over an absolute fame ranking.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frame_(painting)

## Keith Haring: Radiant Baby (from Icons)
Haring’s defining Radiant Baby motif is represented by the exact 1990 Icons silkscreen catalogued by his Foundation. This makes the signature image the feature instead of an incidental outdoor sculpture.
Sources: https://www.haring.com/!/art-work/773

## Salvador Dalí: The Persistence of Memory
MoMA describes this as perhaps Dalí’s most famous painting and notes its immediate success; the original 1931 painting is already present.
Sources: https://www.moma.org/collection/artists/1364 · https://www.moma.org/magazine/articles/1386

## Loïs Mailou Jones: Les Fétiches
Jones’s landmark 1938 African-mask painting is the work singled out at length in Smithsonian’s artist biography and collection interpretation, making it a stronger canonical feature than an arbitrary later work.
Sources: https://americanart.si.edu/artist/lo%C3%AFs-mailou-jones-5658 · https://americanart.si.edu/artwork/les-fetiches-31947

## Henri Rousseau: The Sleeping Gypsy
MoMA explicitly calls this one of its most iconic paintings. It is a stronger documented signature-work choice than keeping the tiger merely because it was the original image; The Dream is another major contender, so no unique numerical ranking is claimed.
Sources: https://www.moma.org/magazine/articles/250 · https://www.moma.org/collection/works/80172

## Robert Indiana: LOVE
Use the familiar red-letter, blue-and-green 1966 LOVE canvas in the Indianapolis Museum of Art at Newfields. The official Legacy Initiative records this exact painting; replace the Imperial Love sculpture image rather than relabel it or count the same composition twice.
Sources: https://robertindiana.com/artworks/artworks-items/love5 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TqDstb54LeY

## George Rodrigue: Watchdog
The original Blue Dog painting is a documented turning point and the subject of Ogden Museum’s dedicated exhibition The First Blue Dog. This anchors Rodrigue in his internationally recognized Blue Dog imagery rather than choosing the first available later print.
Sources: https://ogdenmuseum.org/exhibition/the-first-blue-dog/ · https://georgerodrigue.com/blog/before_the_blue_dog/

## Emily Carr: Church at Yuquot Village (formerly The Indian Church)
The AGO identifies this 1929 church painting as an iconic Carr image, and it is a longstanding collection highlight. Its strong institutional recognition supports choosing it over the previously convenient logging or forest images.
Sources: https://ago.ca/agoinsider/art-toronto-ago-collection

## Piet Mondrian: Composition II in Red, Blue, and Yellow
Choose the recognisable 1930 Kunsthaus Zürich composition with the large red field, not a generic Mondrian-style grid or another similarly titled painting. This is a canonical signature image, but primary evidence does not establish a unique most-famous work ahead of Broadway Boogie Woogie; that ambiguity should remain transparent.
Sources: https://collection.kunsthaus.ch/multimedia/5/2455-en.pdf · https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/komposition-mit-rot-blau-und-gelb-piet-mondrian/5QG2Fm_LGUWAHA?hl=en-GB

## Yayoi Kusama: Pumpkin (Naoshima)
Use the iconic seaside Naoshima pumpkin rather than a different pumpkin painting. Benesse identifies this photographed sculpture as the 2022 re-creation installed after damage to the original 1994 Naoshima work; both dates and the version identity remain explicit.
Sources: https://benesse-artsite.jp/en/special2022/pumpkin.html

## Frederic Remington: The Broncho Buster
The Met calls this Remington’s most popular sculpture and the Autry calls it his first and best-known. The current artwork depicts this exact subject; an alternate cast is not treated as another artwork.
Sources: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/11859 · https://collections.theautry.org/mwebcgi/mweb.exe?request=record%3Bid%3DM511135%3Btype%3D101

## Andrew Wyeth: Christina's World
MoMA describes the 1948 painting as one of the most iconic in American art; this is the widely recognised Wyeth signature image already in the gallery.
Sources: https://www.moma.org/magazine/articles/895 · https://www.moma.org/collection/works/78455

## Diego Rivera: The Flower Carrier
A widely recognised Rivera painting in SFMOMA, selected as the school-appropriate feature instead of the Detroit Industry mural, whose full composition includes unclothed figures.
Sources: https://www.sfmoma.org/artwork/35.4516/

## Grant Wood: American Gothic
The Art Institute’s own Essentials Tour calls this one of the most famous American paintings; its original 1930 Wood painting is the established signature image.
Sources: https://www.artic.edu/artworks/6565/american-gothic · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kUA2gAlCZY

## Pablo Picasso: Three Musicians (Philadelphia version)
A major Cubist composition, selected as the school-appropriate Picasso feature instead of Guernica, which depicts suffering and violence. The exact version and its source are identified in the artwork record.
Sources: https://www.philamuseum.org/objects/53963 · https://www.wikiart.org/en/pablo-picasso/all-works

## Heather Galler: White Cat on a Patchwork Quilt
An actual titled work from Galler’s authenticated artist store, chosen to represent her recognizable patterned animal portraits and decorative folk-art vocabulary. No museum or artist evidence establishes a single most-famous Galler work, so the selection is openly representative rather than presented as a proven fame ranking.
Sources: https://heather-galler-art.mailchimpsites.com/ · https://www.heathergallerstudio.com/products/white-cat-on-a-patchwork-quilt

## Jeff Koons: Balloon Dog
The artist’s own site calls Balloon Dog iconic, and the Pinault Collection identifies it as a trademark work. Select the existing blue monumental steel version, not a small merchandise edition or Puppy.
Sources: https://www.jeffkoons.com/artwork/celebration · https://www.jeffkoons.com/shop · https://lesoeuvres.pinaultcollection.com/en/artist/jeff-koons

## Annie Leibovitz: Queen Elizabeth II in the White Drawing Room (2007 portrait)
The 2007 Queen portraits are a famous, classroom-appropriate part of Leibovitz’s work. Choose the exact White Drawing Room portrait published with her interview, corroborating the 2007 royal commission with the Royal Household; this is not a 2016 family photograph, and no universal fame ranking over all her portraits is claimed.
Sources: https://www.vogue.com/article/annie-leibovitz-remembers-photographing-queen-elizabeth-ii · https://www.royal.uk/photographic-portraits-queen-annie-liebovitz-publication-arrangements?page=1

## Andy Warhol: Campbell's Soup Cans
MoMA calls the complete 1962 work the signature work of Warhol’s career. Select the 32-canvas work as one artwork, not a different single-can print or a later package design.
Sources: https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/1517 · https://www.moma.org/collection/works/79809

## Kamal-ol-Molk: Mirror Hall
Mirror Hall is the painter’s canonical court-interior work, distinguished from later landscapes and bird studies. The Golestan Palace World Heritage nomination identifies the painting, and SOAS uses it as the cover image for its Qajar Iran academic programme.
Sources: https://whc.unesco.org/uploads/nominations/1422.pdf · https://www.soas.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2024-05/Idea%20of%20Iran%202024%20-%20Programme_0.pdf · https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/art-in-iran-xi-post-qajar/

## Georges Seurat: A Sunday on La Grande Jatte
The Art Institute identifies this as Seurat’s largest and best-known painting. Select the full finished 1884–1886 painting, not a preparatory study.
Sources: https://www.artic.edu/artworks/27992/a-sunday-on-la-grande-jatte-1884 · https://www.artic.edu/pdf/static/download-my-museum-tour.pdf-layout-42.pdf

## Roy Lichtenstein: Look Mickey
A signature early Pop painting with familiar cartoon characters, selected as the school-appropriate feature instead of the aerial combat in Whaam! Classroom suitability takes priority over an absolute fame ranking.
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look_Mickey

## Wayne Thiebaud: Cakes
Thiebaud’s 1963 Cakes is a National Gallery highlight repeatedly used in the museum’s teaching materials to explain his signature painted desserts and thick paint. It is a canonical subject-and-work choice, rather than simply any painting of food.
Sources: https://www.nga.gov/artworks/72040-cakes · https://www.nga.gov/sites/default/files/2025-09/an_eye_for_art_opt.pdf

## Norval Morrisseau: Man Changing into Thunderbird
ACI explicitly describes this six-panel work as an iconic masterpiece, and the AGO calls it a seminal work. The current image shows the complete six-panel transformation rather than mislabelling one detail as the entire painting.
Sources: https://artbooks.artcanada.com/norval-morrisseau/key-works/man-changing-into-thunderbird · https://ago.ca/agoinsider/icymi-elder-influence

## Mark Rothko: No.61 (Rust and Blue)
No single Rothko canvas has a defensible universal most-famous ranking. Use this established MOCA colour-field painting as the canonical representative, with its exact 1953 identity; the selection is an editorial signature-work choice, not a claim that museum records establish a global rank.
Sources: https://www.moca.org/artworks/no-61-rust-and-blue-brown-blue-brown-on-blue · https://www.moca.org/exhibitions/geometric-forms-and-abstract-images-selections-from-the-permanent-collection

## Augusta Savage: Lift Every Voice and Sing (The Harp)
Savage’s celebrated 1939 World’s Fair monument was her largest work and final major commission, and Smithsonian gives it central attention. The historic photograph represents the original destroyed monument, with no claim that a modern reconstruction is the original.
Sources: https://americanart.si.edu/artist/augusta-savage-4269 · https://www.searchablemuseum.com/art-and-literature-during-segregation/

## Elizabeth Catlett: Sharecropper
Toledo labels Sharecropper probably Catlett’s most famous and iconic image; Dartmouth’s Hood calls it arguably her best-known. The Carter’s public collection supplies the actual linocut image and object metadata.
Sources: https://emuseum.toledomuseum.org/objects/60640/sharecropper · https://hoodmuseum.dartmouth.edu/news/2013/04/recent-acquisitions-elizabeth-catlett-sharecropper-1952-printed-1968 · https://www.cartermuseum.org/collection/sharecropper-20085

## Paul Klee: Twittering Machine
MoMA’s Klee exhibition release identifies Twittering Machine as the most well-known of his whimsical oil-transfer scenarios. It has stronger primary fame evidence than Castle and Sun, while acknowledging other major Klee images such as Senecio and Ad Parnassum.
Sources: https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/press_archives/6369/releases/MOMA_1986_0103_97.pdf

## Prudence Heward: Rollande
Rollande is one of Heward’s canonical key works: ACI documents its immediate critical acclaim, international exhibition history and enduring importance as a forceful female figure. It is a stronger recognized feature than selecting one of the lesser-known landscapes.
Sources: https://artbooks.artcanada.com/prudence-heward/key-works/rollande

## Michelangelo: Dome of Saint Peter’s Basilica
One of Michelangelo’s best-known architectural designs, selected as the school-appropriate feature instead of the nude David. The description credits the architects who completed the dome after Michelangelo’s death.
Sources: https://www.basilicasanpietro.va/en/san-pietro/the-dome · https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dome_of_Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica_(exterior).jpg

## Edvard Munch: The Scream
Norway’s National Museum calls this one of the best-known images in art history. The existing image is its 1893 version, not the later MUNCH painting, pastel or lithograph.
Sources: https://www.nasjonalmuseet.no/en/collection/object/NG.M.00939

## Faith Ringgold: Woman on a Bridge #1 of 5: Tar Beach
Tar Beach is Ringgold’s signature story-quilt image and a focus of Guggenheim collection teaching and exhibitions. Select the 1988 original quilt, not the related Tar Beach#2 print or the children’s-book cover.
Sources: https://www.guggenheim.org/learning-through-art/art-investigation/community · https://www.guggenheim.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Faith-Ringgold-Press-Kit-Full.pdf

## Amy Sherald: Michelle LaVaughn Robinson Obama
Sherald’s 2018 official Michelle Obama portrait is her internationally recognisable breakthrough commission. Use the exact National Portrait Gallery portrait already in the gallery, rather than another sitter or a photograph of the unveiling.
Sources: https://npg.si.edu/object/npg_NPG.2018.15 · https://npg.si.edu/learn/classroom-resource/michelle-obama

## Hokusai: Under the Wave off Kanagawa (The Great Wave)
The British Museum describes The Great Wave as the world’s most famous Japanese woodblock print. Select the actual print design already supplied; different surviving impressions are not separate compositions.
Sources: https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/korenberg_article-for_hokusai%20_edited_volume_final-2020_accessible.pdf · https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/45434

## David Hockney: A Bigger Splash
A Bigger Splash is the canonical 1967 pool painting catalogued by the David Hockney Foundation and Tate. Keep its exact version distinct from The Splash and The Little Splash, which are separate related paintings.
Sources: https://www.thedavidhockneyfoundation.org/artwork/3667 · https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/hockney-a-bigger-splash-t03254

## Wassily Kandinsky: Composition VIII
Composition8 is a signature Kandinsky abstraction, singled out in Guggenheim collection teaching and the story of the collection’s formation. Select the exact 1923 painting, not an earlier Blue Rider landscape simply because that image was available first.
Sources: https://www.guggenheim-bilbao.eus/en/learn/schools/teachers-guides/composition-8 · https://www.guggenheim-bilbao.eus/en/exhibitions/kandinsky-in-context

## Rembrandt: Self-Portrait with Two Circles
A celebrated late Rembrandt self-portrait in Kenwood’s collection, selected as the school-appropriate feature instead of The Night Watch, whose full composition contains firearms. Classroom suitability takes priority over an absolute fame ranking.
Sources: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rembrandt_Self-portrait_(Kenwood).jpg

## Ted Harrison: The Cremation of Sam McGee — cover painting
Harrison’s 1986 Sam McGee illustrations are among his most widely recognized published works. Yukon Arts Centre publishes the original 13-painting collection; this feature uses its cover painting, not an unrelated recent print or a photograph of a book for sale.
Sources: https://yukonartscentre.com/permanent-art-collections/the-cremation-of-sam-mcgee/ · https://kidscanpress.com/product/cremation-of-sam-mcgee/ · https://tedharrison.ca/pages/about

## René Magritte: The Son of Man
A widely recognised Magritte painting, selected as the school-appropriate feature instead of the smoking pipe in The Treachery of Images. Classroom suitability takes priority over an absolute fame ranking.
Sources: https://www.wikiart.org/en/rene-magritte/son-of-man-1964(1)

## I. Rice Pereira: Machine Composition #2
A prominent 1937 machine-age painting highlighted in Smithsonian’s artist collection and paired with its surviving preparatory study. Pereira has no well-supported single most-famous image; this is an explicitly representative major work that communicates her Bauhaus-related interests.
Sources: https://americanart.si.edu/artist/i-rice-pereira-3761 · https://americanart.si.edu/artwork/machine-composition-2-19551

## Romero Britto: Happy Dog
No reliable consensus establishes one Britto work as his single most famous. Choose an authentic studio-listed original featuring his signature cheerful dog, saturated colour, hearts and black outlines, and label it representative rather than inventing a fame ranking for a public bear sculpture.
Sources: https://www.shopbritto.com/collections/original-paintings/products/happy-dog-original-painting-1

## Jackson Pollock: Autumn Rhythm (Number 30)
A canonical 1950 drip painting at the Met, whose collection text explicitly describes its place in the modern-art canon. Lavender Mist and No.5 are also famous; no primary source establishes a universal first-place ranking, and Autumn Rhythm is chosen on documented museum significance rather than on its position in the current array.
Sources: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/488978 · https://assets.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_226_300087806.pdf

## Jean-Michel Basquiat: Horn Players
A major Basquiat painting celebrating jazz musicians, selected as the school-appropriate feature instead of the anatomical and skeletal imagery in Untitled (Skull). Classroom suitability takes priority over an absolute fame ranking.
Sources: https://www.wikiart.org/en/jean-michel-basquiat/horn-players
