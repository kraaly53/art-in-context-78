# Timeline career evidence

Evidence reviewed 18 November 2026. The horizontal Art Time Machine shows 51 approximate creative periods and all 153 existing career moments. Dates represent documented artistic practice, training, public practice, or an explicitly limited work span; the label and basis identify the distinction. They are not automatically birth/death dates, a complete catalogue, continuous annual production, or evidence that overlapping artists met.

An ongoing range ends at this edition’s review year, 2026. Update the evidence and domain together when extending the edition. Narrower documented spans may end before an artist stopped working. Unknown career beginnings remain explicit, including Heather Galler’s practice documented by her own 2009 blog, later publisher record and current commissions site.

No new images are introduced by the timeline. Portraits and featured-work previews use the same fingerprinted school-reviewed local files as profiles; nine artists have an initials/anonymous fallback.

## Jonathan Green
The artist’s studio dates his professional practice from graduation in 1982 and says that he currently paints in Charleston. The open end means ongoing practice, checked November 2026, not a known future completion year.

- https://www.jonathangreenstudios.com/about

## William H. Johnson
Smithsonian documents his European practice from the late 1920s and his final painting series begun in 1945, followed by his last lifetime exhibition in Copenhagen in March 1947. This activity window ends with that exhibition, not with his much later death in 1970.

- https://americanart.si.edu/artist/william-h-johnson-2486

## Leonardo da Vinci
The Met places his principal professional activity in Florence from 1472, then Milan, Rome and France. The Royal Collection documents his work as royal designer in his final years. These are career years rather than his 1452 birth date.

- https://www.metmuseum.org/fr/essays/leonardo-da-vinci-1452-1519
- https://www.rct.uk/collection/stories/leonardo-in-the-royal-collection/the-life-of-leonardo-da-vinci

## Raphael
The National Gallery identifies Raphael as an independent master in 1500. His own practice ended with his death in 1520; later workshop completion of projects is not counted as his personal activity.

- https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/artists/raphael
- https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/catalogues/plazzotta-and-henry-2022/raphael

## Banksy
Moco Museum dates his emergence in Bristol to the early 1990s and explicitly states that he remains an active artist, checked November 2026. The start is decade-level, not a verified first-work year. London Zoo independently documents his 2024 animal mural. His birth year remains unverified.

- https://www.mocomuseum.com/artists/london/banksy-in-london/
- https://www.mocomuseum.com/news/amsterdam/who-is-banksy/
- https://www.londonzoo.org/zoo-stories/news/banksy-london-zoo

## Paul Cézanne
The Met dates his beginning to paint to 1860. The Phillips Collection chronology documents him painting outdoors in October 1906, shortly before his death.

- https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/paul-cezanne-1839-1906
- https://www.phillipscollection.org/sites/default/files/2024-04/up-close-with-paul-cezanne-digital-press-kit-the-phillips-collection_0.pdf

## Frida Kahlo
The Frida Kahlo Museum identifies her 1926 Velvet Dress self-portrait with the beginning of her artistic career and documents her last intervention on Viva la Vida in 1954. The span does not imply uninterrupted work during illness.

- https://www.museofridakahlo.org.mx/museo/
- https://nmwa.org/art/artists/frida-kahlo/

## Keith Haring
The Haring Foundation documents his first solo exhibition in Pittsburgh in 1978 and his final working years through 1990. Earlier childhood drawing and posthumous productions are not counted as professional career endpoints.

- https://www.haring.com/!/about-haring/bio

## Salvador Dalí
Museum chronology dates his first public group exhibition to 1919. The artist’s foundation explicitly dates his last painting to 1983. This painting-centred window is shorter than his lifespan; he continued writing later.

- https://thedali.org/about-the-museum/timeline/
- https://www.salvador-dali.org/en/biografia/

## Loïs Mailou Jones
The MFA dates completion of her professional design training to 1927, after which she worked in textile design. The Hunter Museum explicitly states she continued painting until 1998.

- https://www.mfa.org/exhibitions/lois-mailou-jones
- https://nmwa.org/art/artists/lois-mailou-jones/
- https://www.huntermuseum.org/exhibition/lois-mailou-jones

## Henri Rousseau
MoMA documents Rousseau exhibiting at the Salon des Indépendants from the mid-1880s and painting The Dream in 1910. The 1886 start is the first exhibition milestone used in the existing classroom chronology; it is not a claim about his first-ever drawing.

- https://www.moma.org/artists/5056-henri-rousseau
- https://www.moma.org/collection/works/80172

## Robert Indiana
The artist’s official chronology includes a dated 1957 studio drawing and continues to a 2017 exhibition made during his lifetime. This is a documented career-activity window, including exhibitions; the endpoint does not claim that every work was newly created in 2017.

- https://www.robertindiana.com/artist/biography
- https://www.robertindiana.com/artist/chronology

## George Rodrigue
The artist’s studio identifies Oak on the Bayou (1968) among his early works and provides an archive of paintings made in 2013. The range follows actual documented painting activity, not his 1944 birth year.

- https://georgerodrigue.com/how-a-cajun-legend-became-a-pop-icon/
- https://georgerodrigue.com/blog/archive-nfs/paintings-2010-2013/

## Emily Carr
The Art Canada Institute starts Carr’s early-work period in 1890 and identifies 1937–1942 as her last-paintings period. There was a substantial lull in painting between 1913 and 1927: the bar is a broad span, not a claim of equal or continuous output. She continued writing after she largely stopped painting.

- https://artbooks.artcanada.com/emily-carr/biography

## Dorothea Lange
The International Center of Photography identifies 1913 as her decision to become a professional photographer. The Oakland Museum’s archive runs through 1965; the end includes preparation of her retrospective as well as photographic practice.

- https://www.icp.org/browse/archive/constituents/dorothea-lange
- https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark%3A%2F13030%2Fft3f59n5wt
- https://dorothealange.museumca.org/about/

## Piet Mondrian
The Thyssen museum documents his entry into the Amsterdam academy and early artistic activity in 1892. Kunstmuseum Den Haag identifies Victory Boogie Woogie (1942–1944) as his final work.

- https://www.museothyssen.org/en/collection/artists/mondrian-piet
- https://www.kunstmuseum.nl/en/mondrian

## Yayoi Kusama
Mori Art Museum’s exhibition history documents solo shows in Matsumoto in 1952. Her official biography and studio notice state that she continued producing art until shortly before her death in August 2026. This is a career span, not her 1929–2026 lifespan.

- https://www.mori.art.museum/files/exhibitions/2020/10/12/YayoiKusama.pdf
- https://yayoi-kusama.jp/en/biography/
- https://yayoi-kusama.jp/news4518/

## Frederic Remington
Smithsonian documents his first western journey as an artist in 1881. The Carter Museum holds his 1909 Broncho Buster and describes his painting/sculpture career as cut short by his death that year.

- https://americanart.si.edu/artist/frederic-remington-4002
- https://www.cartermuseum.org/artists/frederic-remington

## Andrew Wyeth
The Thyssen museum identifies his first exhibited work in 1936. Farnsworth’s museum survey explicitly spans watercolours through 2008. The range ends at documented late work, rather than automatically at his death in 2009.

- https://www.museothyssen.org/en/collection/artists/wyeth-andrew
- https://www.farnsworthmuseum.org/exhibition/andrew-wyeth-maine-watercolors-1938-to-2008/

## Diego Rivera
MoMA’s chronology identifies his first solo exhibition in 1907 and major mural activity continuing through 1954. These selected documented endpoints do not claim that he stopped working in 1954; he lived until 1957.

- https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_1979_300369443.pdf
- https://www.moma.org/interactives/exhibitions/2011/rivera/mobile/chronology.html

## Grant Wood
The National Gallery of Art documents professional design work in Chicago from 1913. Whitney’s exhibition records include his final spring paintings in 1941. The bar therefore uses a documented work span, not his birth and death dates.

- https://www.nga.gov/artists/1982-grant-wood
- https://whitney.org/exhibitions/grant-wood/art?section=8&subsection=2

## Pablo Picasso
The Musée Picasso chronology identifies his first shared studio and exhibited First Communion in 1896, and a posthumous exhibition presented his late works made in 1970–1972. This excludes childhood drawing and does not substitute his birth/death dates for a career.

- https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/
- https://www.museoreinasofia.es/en/exhibition/picasso-grabador-colecciones-museo-nacional-centro-arte-reina-sofia/

## Heather Galler
Her artist blog documents original paintings offered in 2009. A publisher’s 2017 book record and her current official commission site provide later anchors. The beginning of her career is unverified, so 2009 marks a documented practice already under way, with an ongoing period capped at this edition’s 2026 review.

- https://heathergaller.blogspot.com/
- https://walterfoster.com/walter-foster-book/folk-art-fusion/
- https://heather-galler-art.mailchimpsites.com/

## Jeff Koons
SFMOMA and the Whitney document his Inflatable Flowers work in 1979. His official biography documents ongoing professional activity in 2026. The open end is capped at the review year for display and is not a future completion date.

- https://www.sfmoma.org/artist/Jeff_Koons/
- https://whitney.org/exhibitions/jeff-koons/art
- https://www.jeffkoons.com/biography

## Annie Leibovitz
The National Portrait Gallery documents her photographic practice from 1970, and her representing gallery records current books, exhibitions and professional events into 2025–2026. The range covers photographic and related career activity; it does not claim she created every exhibited photograph in its exhibition year.

- https://www.npg.org.uk/collections/search/person/mp08186/annie-leibovitz
- https://www.hauserwirth.com/artists/24984-annie-leibovitz/
- https://saltzmanfamilyfoundation.squarespace.com/saltzman-leibovitz-prize

## Andy Warhol
The Warhol Museum dates his commercial-art career to his move to New York in 1949. It traces his continued painting, films, commissions and collaborations to the Last Supper exhibition in early 1987, just before his death.

- https://www.warhol.org/andy-warhols-life/

## Kamal-ol-Molk
The specialist catalogue survey identifies dated paintings from about 1880 and final works in the early 1930s. The upper plot boundary represents that approximate decade, not an exact final-work year. This is earlier than his death in 1940.

- https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kamal-al-molk-mohammad-gaffari/kamal-al-molk-mo%E1%B8%A5ammad-%E1%B8%A1affari-iii-works/

## Georges Seurat
Begins with documented art-school work in 1878; his paintings continued through The Circus in 1891. Includes early training, rather than pretending that his public breakthrough in 1884 was his first creative work.

- https://www.guggenheim.org/artwork/artist/Georges-Seurat
- https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/artists/georges-seurat

## Roy Lichtenstein
The Roy Lichtenstein Foundation describes its study collection as covering his entire career, 1940–1997. This includes work before his familiar comic-inspired Pop paintings.

- https://lichtensteinfoundation.org/press/
- https://www.lichtensteincatalogue.org/chronology/

## Wayne Thiebaud
The Crocker exhibition traces his career from commercial art in the 1940s through his late work. The beginning is a decade-level approximation, not a claim of a first artwork made in 1940.

- https://www.crockerart.org/press/wayne-thiebaud-a-celebration-1920-2021
- https://www.crockerart.org/art/artists/wayne-thiebaud

## Norval Morrisseau
Art Canada Institute documents paintings and professional support around 1958 and his participation in a major retrospective in 2006. Illness reduced his later production; the interval does not claim that he made new paintings every year or that 2006 was his final painting date.

- https://artbooks.artcanada.com/norval-morrisseau/biography

## Mark Rothko
Begins with his move to New York and art classes in 1923, rather than his birth. The National Gallery of Art holds paintings made in 1970, confirming creative work at the far end of the interval.

- https://www.nga.gov/artists/1839-mark-rothko
- https://www.nga.gov/sites/default/files/migrate_images/content/dam/ngaweb/exhibitions/pdfs/artsince1950.pdf

## Augusta Savage
Smithsonian accounts place the start of her New York sculptural career around 1920 and her retreat from the public art world in the early-to-mid 1940s. Sources disagree on the move date (1940 or 1945); this is an approximate public-career band. She made fewer works and continued teaching afterward, so neither 1945 nor her death in 1962 is asserted as an exact final-artwork date.

- https://americanart.si.edu/artist/augusta-savage-4269
- https://americanart.si.edu/education/oh-freedom/augusta-savage

## Elizabeth Catlett
Begins with completion of her art degree and entry into art education in 1935. The Smithsonian records that she lived and worked between Mexico and New York until 2012; this band includes teaching, printmaking and sculpture.

- https://nmaahc.si.edu/latinx/elizabeth-catlett
- https://nmwa.org/art/artists/elizabeth-catlett/

## Paul Klee
Zentrum Paul Klee dates his first reverse-glass paintings and etchings to 1902–1906. Its chronology records his prolific late work in 1939; dated works in 1940, including Death and Fire, establish the end of this creative span.

- https://www.zpk.org/en/paul-klee-biography
- https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/paul-klee-1879-1940

## Prudence Heward
Uses her early-1920s professional practice after returning to Montreal and her last painting year, 1945. Childhood drawings and earlier training are not treated as the beginning of her main career; health caused interruptions within this interval.

- https://artbooks.artcanada.com/prudence-heward/biography
- https://artbooks.artcanada.com/prudence-heward/credits

## Michelangelo
Casa Buonarroti dates an early relief to about 1490. The Castello Sforzesco documents work on his final sculpture until days before his death in 1564. Later completion dates on architecture by collaborators do not extend Michelangelo’s own activity beyond 1564.

- https://www.casabuonarroti.it/en/museum/collections/michelangelos-works/madonna-della-scala/
- https://www2.comune.milano.it/web/castello-sforzesco/i-musei/museo-pieta-rondanini-michelangelo/la-pieta-rondanini

## Edvard Munch
MUNCH’s chronology records art-school drawing and his decision to paint in 1880, and a watercolour responding to an event in December 1943. The final boundary is documented creative activity, not an assumption that he painted throughout his final illness in 1944.

- https://www.munch.no/en/edvard-munch/edvard-munch-timeline/

## Faith Ringgold
Smithsonian records her painting while teaching by 1955. Miami MoCAAD identifies Ancestors Part II, 2017, extending the documented work span beyond the gallery’s 2011 work. This conservative interval is not her entire lifetime or a claim that she stopped creating in 2017.

- https://americanart.si.edu/artist/faith-ringgold-7236
- https://www.miamimocaad.org/artists/faith-ringgold

## Amy Sherald
The High Museum’s 2026 mid-career retrospective documents paintings from 2007 to 2024 and an active contemporary practice. The starting year is the earliest work in that survey, not her first art lesson or entire career; present is capped at the site’s 2026 review date.

- https://high.org/exhibition/amy-sherald-american-sublime/
- https://nmwa.org/art/artists/amy-sherald/

## Hokusai
Museum sources document his first actor prints and studio career in 1779 and paintings made in his last year, 1849. Apprenticeship and childhood drawing precede this professional-publication span.

- https://www.ngv.vic.gov.au/exhibition/hokusai/
- https://www.tmja.org.il/eng/Exhibitions/510/Hokusai

## Jacob Lawrence
The catalogue raisonné explicitly covers paintings, drawings and murals from 1935 to 1999. The interval follows documented creative production and does not use his 1917 birth or 2000 death as automatic career boundaries.

- https://uwapress.uw.edu/book/9780295979632/the-complete-jacob-lawrence/
- https://blogs.rollins.edu/rma/2020/09/23/a-minor-jacob-lawrence-mystery-solved/

## David Hockney
The artist’s foundation records paintings and sketchbooks from Bradford art-school years beginning in 1953, later painting and digital work, and recent work shown in 2026. The foundation confirms his death on 11 June 2026; do not mark him as a currently living artist.

- https://www.thedavidhockneyfoundation.org/chronology/1953
- https://www.thedavidhockneyfoundation.org/chronology/2026

## Wassily Kandinsky
Begins when he left law for art study in Munich in 1896, including the training period. Museum biographies document later abstract work and his final Paris period through 1944.

- https://www.museothyssen.org/en/collection/artists/kandinsky-wassily
- https://www.guggenheim.org/artwork/artist/vasily-kandinsky

## Rembrandt
The Rijksmuseum identifies 1625 as the year of his first dated work, and the Mauritshuis identifies a self-portrait made in 1669. Training predates this independently working, documented artistic span.

- https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/stories/themes/rembrandt/story/10-things-about-life-rembrandt

## Ted Harrison
The artist’s official biography identifies his first Canadian exhibition in 1969 and documents his public art activity through the 2009 donation of Vast Yukon. This is a documented Canadian career span, not his entire practice: art study and teaching began earlier, and an exact final-work date is not established.

- https://tedharrison.ca/pages/about

## René Magritte
Includes his Brussels art-academy years beginning in 1916 and his sustained later painting and research. The museum biography places his final career in the 1960s; the range is an approximate broad career period rather than a list of each year worked.

- https://assets.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_1898_300062306.pdf

## I. Rice Pereira
